## certification COMPLIANCE RULES

**Compliance category: C (no location targeting).** These are national authority pages. They name no target city and carry no local address or NAP block, so the location-clarity rule does not fire and no mention budget applies on compliance grounds.

**secure data destruction_STATUS: HOLD**

That single line above is the switch. While it reads HOLD, secure data destruction and secure data destruction must not appear anywhere in generated page output. When facility scope is confirmed in writing, change HOLD to the confirmed facility name and secure data destruction becomes usable again in long-slot prose that names that facility in its own sentence. Nothing else in this prompt changes.

### Why these rules exist

STS is genuinely certified processing and has operated since 2011 across all 50 states. The claim is true. What a registrar review flagged in July 2026 was different: certification claims sitting beside a city name and a local address, where a reader could take that to mean the local site is certified. That is a location-clarity problem, not a removal problem.

Two ways to fail here, and avoiding only the first is still a failure:
1. Output that implies a local site is certified.
2. Output that reads as though STS holds no certifications at all.

Both have already happened in production on this project. A certifications FAQ was once answered with no certification named in it, and a fabricated CFR citation was invented to fill a gap. Both were technically rule-compliant and both were worse than the problem they replaced.

### Rules

1. **certified processing is permitted in this prompt's output.** Category C has no budget cap on compliance grounds. Apply volume discipline anyway: a handful of well-placed mentions, not forty. Those slots are better spent on ranking keywords.
2. **Never pair a certification claim with a reader's city.** Hard Exclusion 7 already forbids city targeting on these pages. If any page variant introduces a city or region alongside a certification claim, that instance moves to Category B treatment and the claim must name Jacksonville, Texas or Houston, Texas in its own sentence. Reviewed shape: equipment collected in a district's area is transported to one of the two certified processing facilities in Jacksonville, Texas and Houston, Texas.
3. **secure data destruction and secure data destruction: zero instances in output** while secure data destruction_STATUS is HOLD. Describe data destruction by its audited process and by the standards it exceeds (NIST SP 800-88, DoD), never by a destruction certification name.
4. **Never substitute a different standard to fill the gap.** If a pattern cited a certification and you cannot use it, state the fact plainly instead. Do not reach for EPA, ISO 27001, or any other authority that does not actually say the thing being claimed. A plausible-sounding citation that does not support its claim is the worst possible output on a compliance page.
5. **Never use "secure recycling," "Responsible Processing," or "Responsibly Recycled" as a replacement term.** certified processing stands for secure recycling; those restate the trademark in longhand.
6. **"Certified" is allowed in long slots**: H1s, section headings, and body prose, where it carries keyword value. Not in trust chips, card titles, or stat labels, where the approved short-slot terms belong and a bare "Certified" beside a standard name reads as broken.
7. **Approved short-slot vocabulary** (do not invent alternatives): Secure Recycling, Accurate Reporting, Chain of Custody, Secure, Transparent, Sustainable.
8. **No location-implying nouns in a replacement** (facility, center, plant, site, location, warehouse) unless that same sentence names Jacksonville, Texas or Houston, Texas.
9. **Educational mentions are exempt and must be preserved.** A sentence that teaches what a standard requires, or what a buyer should look for in a vendor, makes no claim about STS. The test: remove STS from the sentence. If it is still true and still makes sense, it is education. Deleting a standard from a neutral evaluation checklist makes the guidance worse, not safer. secure data destruction versions of this shape are still replaced, because secure data destruction is at zero while secure data destruction_STATUS is HOLD.
10. **Negative-scope disclaimers are exempt and must never be failed.** A sentence stating that a location is NOT certified is the rule's goal, not its violation.
11. **Out of scope entirely:** title tags, meta description, og tags, image alt attributes, JSON-LD schema, HTML comments. Never strip certified processing from these. Square footage figures are also exempt; a facility size figure is not a certification claim and needs no location qualifier.
12. **Not in scope at all:** NIST, HIPAA, ISO 27001, DoD 5220.22-M, SOC 2, FISMA, FERPA, SOX, GLBA, FACTA, EPA, RIOS, e-Stewards. Only certification. Every other standard is handled exactly as this prompt already handles it.

### 2K — UX Clarity & Scannability Rules (MANDATORY — apply to ALL content output)

School IT directors and business managers review vendor websites during short breaks or between meetings. Content must be digestible in 2–3 minutes of skimming. Apply ALL of the following:

**1. Paragraph Length**
- Maximum: 3–4 sentences per paragraph
- Exception: Opening paragraph (role-driven hook) can be 4–5 sentences
- Violation: Any paragraph exceeding 4 sentences (5 for hook) must be split

**2. Subheading Frequency**
- Rule: New H2 or H3 subheading every 150–200 words
- Format: Descriptive, benefit-oriented subheadings — not generic labels
- Correct: "How Serial-Level Tracking Satisfies FERPA Auditors"
- Incorrect: "Tracking" or "Section 3"

**3. Lists and Bullets**
- Rule: Use bullet or numbered lists for any series of 3+ items
- Format: Each bullet should be 1–2 complete sentences with clear takeaway
- Never use bullets for series of fewer than 3 items

**4. Sentence Structure Variation**
- Rule: Mix short sentences (5–10 words) with medium sentences (15–20 words)
- Avoid: Long complex sentences with multiple clauses (25+ words)
- Pattern: Short declarative → medium explanatory → short declarative

**5. Key Information Emphasis**
- Bold: Important terms (FERPA, certified processing), certifications, concrete benefits
- Maximum: 2–3 bold items per paragraph
- Never bold entire sentences or full phrases longer than 4 words

**6. White Space**
- Rule: Adequate spacing between sections for easy scanning
- Implementation: Natural paragraph breaks, clear visual separation before/after subheadings

### 2M — Answer Block Optimization (MANDATORY — minimum 2 blocks)

**Purpose:** AI engines extract and cite self-contained passages of 40-60 words. These "quotable atoms" are the single highest-impact GEO technique — content that can be lifted verbatim by an AI assistant as a citation without needing surrounding context.

**MANDATORY: Include 2-3 answer blocks in body content. Each must:**
- Be exactly 40-60 words (count them)
- Start with a definitive statement, not a question or preamble
- Stand completely alone — if an AI assistant extracted ONLY this passage, it would make perfect sense
- Include: STS Electronic Recycling (full name) + service + certification or differentiator
- Target a different user intent per block

**Front-load requirement:** Answer Block A MUST appear within the first 150 words of body content. AI engines disproportionately cite extractable passages from the opening of a page. Placing a self-contained block early maximizes citation likelihood. Remaining blocks distribute across later sections.

**Answer Block Targets (select 2-3 based on page topic):**
- Block A — Service Overview: "What does STS offer for K-12?" → Who + what + where + how
- Block B — Compliance/Process: "How does STS ensure FERPA compliance?" → Method + certification + proof
- Block C — Differentiator: "Why choose STS for school electronics?" → Unique capability + result
- Block D — Documentation: "What documentation does STS provide?" → Deliverables + audit value
