# REALISTIC IMAGE/VIDEO PROMPT v1.0

Paste-first starting point for every image and video generation.
Contains no scene, subject, vertical, city, or service. Those arrive in the
scene brief that follows this block.

---

## 0. HOW TO USE

Paste this prompt first. Paste the scene brief second.

If pasted alone into a conversational model, do not generate anything.
Respond only with: `Base prompt loaded. Awaiting scene brief.` and stop.

If concatenated with a scene brief into a direct generation call, this prompt
governs the whole call.

Conflict rule: if the scene brief contradicts Section 2 (output contract),
Section 5 (prohibitions), or Section 7 (video), this prompt wins. Say which
line you overrode. Do not silently comply with the brief.

---

## 1. OPERATOR HEADER

Fill every field before use. Blank fields stop the run. Do not guess.

```
MEDIUM:    image | video
REGISTER:  A | B | C        (Section 3)
ASPECT:    16:9 | 4:5 | 9:16 | 3:2 | 1:1
PURPOSE:   where this ships (hero, band, inline, social, print)
```

---

## 2. OUTPUT CONTRACT

One frame. One scene. One generation.

Never produce a collage, grid, contact sheet, multi-panel layout, split
screen, before-and-after comparison, numbered layout, gallery, or storyboard
sheet. Never add a border, frame overlay, matte, caption bar, or logo bug.

Never combine two scene types into one crowded frame. If the brief describes
two scenes, generate the first and flag the second.

Honor the declared aspect ratio natively. Do not letterbox, pillarbox, or pad
to reach it.

Generate at full native detail on the first pass. Do not produce a soft frame
with the intent of upscaling it afterward.

---

## 3. REGISTER

Pick exactly one. Never blend elements across registers. The register
determines the capture spec, so do not hand-write a capture spec.

### A. PHONE-DOCUMENTARY

Use for anything a customer, homeowner, technician, or field worker would
plausibly film themselves. This is the default.

Capture: modern flagship phone main camera, roughly 26mm equivalent, standard
phone HDR handling the ambient light, deep depth of field, faint cool auto
white balance fighting the ambient, crisp phone-level sharpness that is never
artificially oversharpened, continuous handheld micro-movement consistent with
a person standing and holding a phone.

Forbidden in this register: shallow depth of field, bokeh balls, anamorphic
flare, cinematic color grade, film grain overlay, gimbal smoothness, drone
movement, digital zoom artifacts.

### B. CORPORATE-COMMISSIONED

Use only when the frame must read as a photo the company paid a photographer
to take. Homepage marquee, trade show wall, printed collateral.

Capture: full-frame body, 24 to 70mm f/2.8 zoom, f/5.6 to f/11, ISO 100 to
400, corrected verticals, minimal barrel distortion, natural highlight
rolloff, fine sensor grain, restrained sharpening.

Lighting is professional but not even. Accept and specify one blown window or
dock door, one dim corner, one hot spot on a floor or metal surface. Perfectly
even illumination is a marketing preference, not a photographic one, and it
reads as AI.

### C. EDITORIAL-DOCUMENTARY

A working photojournalist inside a real operation. Sits between A and B. Use
when B reads too polished and A too casual.

Capture: full-frame body, 35mm prime, available light only, f/4 to f/8,
slight underexposure to protect highlights, visible natural grain at ISO 800
to 1600, imperfect framing consistent with reacting to a moment rather than
composing one.

### Register warning

B is the register current models fake best and viewers reject fastest.
Balanced composition, even light, and uniform sharpness are the signature
people now read as AI. Default to A or C unless the deliverable specifically
requires B.

---

## 4. THE SIX LEVERS

Every generation answers all six with a specific. A category is a failed
answer. "Add natural imperfections" is a failed answer. Name the object, name
the flaw, name the hour.

**1. Reason the frame exists.** Who is holding the camera and why they raised
it. This fixes vantage height, distance, and angle better than any measurement.

**2. Off-center subject.** State where the subject sits in the frame and what
occupies the remaining space. Never dead center. Never mirror-symmetrical.
Never mathematically even spacing.

**3. Frame-edge cut.** Name one object the crop slices through: a pallet
corner, a shoulder, a hanging cable, a mirror, a doorframe. Real photographs
cut things. Generated ones politely contain everything.

**4. Named defect.** One specific flaw on one specific object. Not "worn
pallets." Instead: "the third pallet from camera has a cracked bottom board
and sits half an inch lower on the left."

**5. Uncooperative light.** One thing the light is doing that a photographer
would not have chosen. A window blowing out behind the subject. A fixture
reflecting off a monitor. A shadow falling across half a face. Light that
behaves perfectly is the fastest AI tell after hands.

**6. Time and weather commitment.** An exact hour and a sky condition. Every
shadow length, shadow direction, color temperature, and reflection follows
from it. "Daylight" and "natural light" are failed answers.

---

## 5. ABSOLUTE PROHIBITIONS

No text rendered inside the frame. No letters, numbers, captions, labels,
signage, license plates, screen text, pallet stamps, hand-lettering, or
watermarks. Screens may show generic graphics, charts, or interface shapes
with no legible characters.

No logos or brand marks, real or invented, on any surface, garment, vehicle,
or equipment.

No certification marks, badges, wall certificates, or compliance seals of any
kind, real or lookalike. A visible cert mark inside an image is a compliance
claim, and compliance claims are never made in a picture.

No recognizable landmark, skyline, monument, or city signage. Interiors and
exteriors are location-agnostic by design. Page copy, filename, and alt text
carry local intent. The image never claims to be somewhere.

No identifiable real person. No depiction of a real named company's premises.

Generated imagery only. No stock sourcing of any kind.

Nothing unsafe on camera. No exposed cutting blades, no unguarded moving
machinery, no one lifting beyond plausible human capacity, no fire, smoke,
sparks, flying debris, blood, or injury.

---

## 6. NEGATIVE BLOCK

Use this short block. Do not append a long legacy negative list to it.

```
AI-generated look, CGI, 3D render, plastic skin, waxy oversmoothed faces,
neon oversaturation, oversharpened halos, repeating identical objects,
warped geometry, floating or disconnected objects, readable text,
collage or grid layout
```

Status: this short block replaces the legacy 200-term blocks pending a scored
bake-off. Rationale is that current models expose no separate negative field,
so appended negatives are read as prompt content, and naming anatomy failures
may raise attention on them rather than lower it. That rationale is untested
on this stack. Retain the legacy blocks in their source files until the
bake-off is scored. Do not delete them on the strength of this note.

---

## 7. VIDEO ONLY

**Continuity method is declared before any multi-shot sequence.** Either a
reference image passed into every generation, or a last-frame chain where each
clip seeds the next. Text alone cannot hold continuity across separate
generations, because the model generating shot two has never seen shot one.
If no method is declared, generate a single shot and stop.

**One camera move per clip, maximum.** A camera move plus a subject action
plus a lighting change in one clip is where models break. Pick the move or
pick the action.

**No on-screen text, ever.** Titles, tips, callouts, phone numbers, URLs, and
end cards are added in an editor after generation. Asking a video model to
render a phone number is the most reliable way to produce garbled characters.
Instead, compose the frame to reserve clean uncluttered space where the
overlay will sit later, and say which third of the frame that is.

**Do not specify durations in seconds** unless the target model accepts a
duration parameter. Instead describe an action that completes in one
continuous take.

**Physics is stated, not assumed.** Say how water arcs, beads, and darkens the
surface it lands on. Say how fabric loads and releases. Say that each blade,
cable, or strand moves independently with its own mass. Reject any output
where a surface moves as one connected mesh.

**Lighting continuity across a sequence.** One sun position, one color
temperature, one shadow direction across every clip. No mid-clip shift in
direction or temperature.

---

## 8. ACCEPTANCE TEST

Run on every frame before it ships. No judgment required. Any single failure
is a reject and regenerate.

1. Hands: every visible hand has five countable, correctly jointed fingers.
2. Text: zero readable characters anywhere in frame, including screens,
   labels, and signage.
3. Cert marks: no badge, seal, certificate, or logo anywhere.
4. Repetition: no two objects identical in shape, wear, and spacing.
5. Shadows: every shadow agrees on one light direction.
6. Reflections: every reflective surface shows what is actually in front of it.
7. Contact: every object and person meets the floor with a shadow at the
   contact point. Nothing floats.
8. Geometry: verticals straight, no melted or merged equipment, no impossible
   ports, seams, or fasteners.
9. Composition: subject is off-center and at least one object is cut by the
   frame edge.
10. Layout: single frame, no grid, no border, no caption.

Two-attempt rule: if the same scene fails twice on the primary generator,
escalate to the fallback generator once, then stop and flag for the operator.
Never ship a failed frame to fill a slot.
