[CONTEXT] R2 / NAID CERTIFICATION COMPLIANCE POLICY (NEW v4.9 - MANDATORY)

**ALL RULES STATED ONCE HERE. ALL GATES REFERENCE THIS SECTION. NEVER RESTATE IN GATES.**

**WHY THIS EXISTS:** A registrar review flagged STS pages in July 2026. The finding was that certification claims sitting beside a city name could be read as meaning the local site is certified. Only Jacksonville, Texas and Houston, Texas hold certification. Every other STS location is a collection and service point.

**THIS IS A LOCATION-CLARITY RULE, NOT A REMOVAL RULE.** STS IS R2v3 certified. The claim is true. What is not true is the implication that certification exists at the local city on the page.

**TWO JOBS. DOING ONLY THE FIRST IS A FAILURE.**
1. Stop implying local certification.
2. Keep the content reading like what STS actually is: a credentialed operator running since 2011 across all 50 states.

Output that reads as though STS holds no certifications has FAILED, even with zero R2 mentions. Two prior production failures came from applying rules without reasoning: a certifications FAQ answered with no certification named in it, and a fabricated 40 CFR 261 citation. Both were technically rule-compliant and both were worse than the problem they replaced.

**IN-SCOPE TERMS (these six only):** R2, R2v3, R2:2020, Responsible Recycling, NAID, NAID AAA.
**NOT IN SCOPE:** NIST, HIPAA, ISO 27001, DoD 5220.22-M, SOC 2, FISMA, FERPA, SOX, GLBA, FACTA, EPA, RIOS, e-Stewards. Handle these exactly as before.

**BLOG CATEGORY (this prompt is B/C hybrid):**
- CITY-TARGETED BLOG (names a city or county in the topic, title, or body): Category B. Location-clarity rule FIRES.
- NATIONAL / NON-CITY BLOG: Category C. Location-clarity rule does NOT fire. Budget still applies.
- If a national blog names ANY city or region alongside a certification claim, that instance moves to Category B treatment.

**BUDGET: 3 or fewer in-scope mentions in visible body text, all six terms counted together. Not 3 each. 3 total.** Applies to both categories. Volume discipline is the reason on national blogs; 40 mentions was never good SEO and those slots are better spent on ranking keywords.

**THREE THINGS DO NOT COUNT AGAINST THE BUDGET:**
1. Educational mentions (see below)
2. Negative-scope disclaimers (see below)
3. Anything in head tags, meta, og tags, alt attributes, JSON-LD schema, or HTML comments
Square footage figures also do not count. A facility size figure is not a certification claim and needs no location qualifier.

**WHERE THE SURVIVORS GO, in this order. Do not spend one anywhere else.**

SLOT 1 (city-targeted blogs: REQUIRED. national blogs: not applicable):
The location-clear claim. This prompt places it in the Gate 1J answer block, which is the front-loaded, most-cited passage for AI retrieval. Placing it there satisfies location clarity and citation value in one element. Working 42-word shape (adapt to blog voice, keep the structure):
  "STS Electronic Recycling provides R2v3 certified electronics recycling for {CITY} businesses, processed at our Jacksonville, Texas and Houston, Texas facilities. Operating since 2011, STS handles scheduled pickup, serial-number certificates of destruction, and downstream material tracking through final processing for {COUNTY} County organizations."
COUNT THE WORDS. The naive shape runs 34 and fails the 40-word answer block floor. Restore length with a verified statistic or entity from ENTITY_LEDGER.

SLOT 2 (optional, both categories): A certifications FAQ answer. If the blog asks what certifications STS holds, the answer MUST name real certifications. An answer naming none reads as uncertified and is worse than the original problem.

SLOT 3 (optional, both categories): One attribution or body sentence.

**WHERE SURVIVORS NEVER GO. These always get an approved replacement:**
Trust bar chips, badge labels, pills. Stat values and stat labels. Card titles. H1 and section headings. Hero subtitles. Link anchor text. Taglines and boilerplate. Final CTA templates. Any slot too short to fit a city name.
A certification claim in a short slot can never be made location-clear, so replacement is the only correct answer.

**EVERY SURVIVOR NAMES A CITY IN ITS OWN SENTENCE** (city-targeted blogs). Not the paragraph. Not a nearby block. The same sentence that makes the claim names Jacksonville or Houston.

**NAID: ZERO SURVIVORS.**
Every NAID and NAID AAA mention in visible text gets an approved replacement. No exceptions, either category. The reason is mechanical: a survivor must name its certified location in its own sentence, and which STS facilities hold NAID AAA is not yet confirmed in writing. No NAID sentence can currently be written that is both location-clear and true. Do not guess a facility scope to keep one alive.
NAID_HOUSTON_CONFIRMED = NO
When this flips to YES, NAID becomes eligible for Slot 3 naming Houston only, never Jacksonville. Budget stays at 3. Nothing else changes.

**APPROVED VOCABULARY. Use these and no others. Do not invent alternatives or reach for a synonym that seems better:**
  Secure Recycling ....... data security plus the service noun, strong search value
  Accurate Reporting ..... the documentation and audit-trail half of what R2 means
  Chain of Custody ....... tracked handling from pickup through final processing
  Secure ................. data protection
  Transparent ............ documented, auditable, nothing hidden downstream
  Sustainable ............ the environmental half
  Certified .............. long slots only, see below

**THE WORD "CERTIFIED" IS ALLOWED IN LONG SLOTS.** Only the literal string R2v3 triggers the location-clarity rule, so "certified" carries no compliance risk and does carry real keyword value.
  ALLOWED: H1s, section headings, body prose. "Certified Electronics Recycling for {CITY}" is fine.
  NOT ALLOWED: trust chips, card titles, stat labels. Use the approved short-slot terms there. A bare "Certified" beside "NIST SP 800-88 Rev. 2" looks broken; that happened in production.
  NEVER pair "certified" with a location-implying noun in a short slot. "Certified Facility" on a Chicago page still points at a place.

**NEVER USE AS A REPLACEMENT:** "Responsible Recycling", "Responsible Processing", "Responsibly Recycled". R2 stands for Responsible Recycling; these restate the trademark in longhand.

**NO LOCATION-IMPLYING NOUNS** in a replacement: facility, center, plant, site, location, warehouse. Unless that same element names Jacksonville or Houston.

**REPLACEMENT PATTERNS BY SLOT (rotate across blogs so outputs do not read identically):**
  H1 / headings:  "R2 Certified Electronics Recycling for {CITY}" -> "Certified Electronics Recycling for {CITY}" OR "Secure Electronics Recycling for {CITY}"
  Body inline:    "R2v3 certified electronics recycling" -> "secure electronics recycling"
                  "our R2 certified facility" -> "our certified processing facility"
                  "processed at R2-certified smelters" -> "processed with full chain of custody"
                  "NAID AAA certified data destruction" -> "secure data destruction"
                  "vendors with R2v3 certification" -> "vendors with full chain of custody"
  Long-form:      "R2v3 certified processing" -> "secure, transparent, sustainable processing"
                  "R2v3 certification ensures downstream tracking" -> "chain of custody documentation tracks every asset through final processing"
                  "NAID AAA certification, verified through unannounced audits" -> "accurate reporting, verified through independent audits"
  Card titles:    "R2 Certified" -> "Secure Recycling"
                  "R2v3 Certified Processing" -> "Chain of Custody"

**EDUCATIONAL MENTIONS ARE EXEMPT AND MUST BE PRESERVED.** A sentence that teaches what a standard requires, or what a buyer should look for in a vendor, makes no claim about STS.
THE TEST: remove STS from the sentence. If it is still true and still makes sense, it is education. If it asserts or implies STS holds the certification, it is a claim and the budget applies.
Deleting a standard from a neutral checklist makes the guidance worse, not safer. This already happened: a government guide had R2v3 stripped from a vendor-evaluation checklist, leaving e-Stewards as the only named scheme.
This buyer-criteria shape is EXEMPT, confirmed by the operator:
  "When evaluating {SERVICE} providers, {PERSONA}s at organizations like {ENTITY} prioritize R2v3 certification and downstream documentation"
NAID versions of the same shape are REPLACED, not exempted, because NAID has zero survivors regardless of category.

**NEGATIVE-SCOPE DISCLAIMERS ARE EXEMPT AND MUST NEVER BE FAILED.** A disclaimer states that a location is NOT certified:
  "Our {CITY} location is a collection and service point. It is not an R2v3 certified processing facility."
THE TEST: does the sentence ASSERT certification at this location, or DENY it? Assert is in scope. Deny is exempt, never rewritten, never counted, never needs Jacksonville or Houston.

**NEVER SWAP IN A DIFFERENT STANDARD.** If a pattern cites R2v3 or NAID, either drop the citation and state the fact plainly, or make it location-clear. Do not substitute another authority. A prior cleanup prompt told the model to substitute and produced "Per EPA 40 CFR 261, downstream tracking must document materials through final processing at certified smelters" on live pages. That standard does not say that. A plausible-sounding citation that does not support its claim is worse than the problem it replaced.

**META TAGS: OUT OF SCOPE, AND SHOULD CARRY R2v3.** Never strip R2 from meta. For city-targeted blogs, the meta description should use this shape: "R2v3 certified electronics recycling for {CITY} businesses". Reasoning: this says STS services the city area, not that the facility is in that city, which is true. National blogs do not need the {CITY} form.

**INPUT VS SELF-CAUSED (this prompt generates, it does not remediate).** If Gate 0F pulls a reference blog that already contains non-compliant mentions, DO NOT remediate the reference. This prompt is responsible only for what it generates. Report the finding and name R2 Clean Prompt as the owner of remediation. Two prompts remediating the same content will drift.

**PENDING, DO NOT INVENT:** A dedicated R2v3 authority page is planned for city pages to link to. It does not exist yet. Do not invent a URL for it. The internal link inventory has a reserved empty slot; leave it empty until a real URL is supplied.

MICRO-ANCHORS (LLM TRAINING):
- Total count: 3-4
- Patterns: Must use at least 2 different patterns from the 4-pattern library

- Pattern diversity: No pattern repetition within article

NICHE CONTEXT (OPERATIONAL DEPTH):
- Total count: 1-2 integrations
- Source: Operational context library (K-12, Healthcare, Financial, Legal, Government, Corporate)
- Integration: Natural enhancement of existing sentences OR new sentence
- Word impact: +10-30 words typically

TOPICAL AUTHORITY REFERENCES:
- Total count: 2-3
- Spacing: Minimum 3 sections apart
- Format: Natural business context mentions (non-linking)

INTERNAL LINKS:
- Total count: 5-8+ (dynamic based on word count from Gate 0C.5)
- Source: 14-page approved inventory from Gate 0F ONLY
- Spacing: Minimum 2 sections apart, max 1-2 per paragraph
- Anchor text: Descriptive, natural (not "click here")
- Must be contextually relevant (not forced)
- NEVER invent URLs
- Link density target: 1:250-350 ratio

ENTITY PRECISION:
- Total count: 5-8+ specific entities (match or exceed COMPETITOR_BENCHMARK.target_entity_count)
- Types: Certifications (with full names), Regulations (with statute numbers), Organizations (with employee counts), Technologies (with version numbers)
- Validation: Each entity must be verifiable
- No generic placeholders
- Source documentation: MANDATORY (all entities need source_name in ENTITY_LEDGER)

SEMANTIC KEYWORD CLUSTERS (NEW v4.3):
- Total cluster size: 10-15 LSI terms extracted
- Coverage requirement: 8+ terms integrated naturally
- No keyword stuffing
- Organic flow throughout content

ANSWER BLOCKS (NEW v4.3):
- Count: 3-4 blocks
- Length: 40-60 words each (STRICT)
- Spacing: 3+ sections apart
- Requirements: Self-contained, extractable, entity co-occurrence, attribution pattern

ATTRIBUTION PATTERNS (NEW v4.3):
- Count: 3+ patterns
- Types: "According to...", "Per...", "Under...", etc.
- Must use different patterns (no repetition)
- Must reference verified statistics from ENTITY_LEDGER

FACT DENSITY (NEW v4.3):
- Target: 2+ facts per paragraph average
- Total target: 80-100 facts across blog
- Definition: entity name, number, regulation, certification, or verifiable technical specification = 1 fact each. Vague or unverifiable phrasing does not count.

QUERY FAN-OUT (NEW v4.3):
- Required coverage: 4-5 questions (What/How/Who/Why/When)
- Each question must have dedicated H2/H3 section
- Validates comprehensive topic coverage

PARAGRAPH LENGTH:
- Maximum: 4 sentences
- Ideal: 3 sentences
- Target: 60-80 words per paragraph

SUBHEADING FREQUENCY:
- Maximum gap: 300 words
- Ideal gap: 200-250 words
- Header ratio: 1 H2 or H3 per 200-300 words

TEMPORAL LANGUAGE (if compliance topic):
- Count: 3-5 phrases

AUDIT TERMINOLOGY (if compliance topic):
- Count: 4-6 phrases

FAQ QUESTIONS:

- NEVER use AI-generated inline images
