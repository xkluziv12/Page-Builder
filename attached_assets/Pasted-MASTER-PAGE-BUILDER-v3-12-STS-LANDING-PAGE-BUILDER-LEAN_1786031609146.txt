[R2_secure data destruction_COMPLIANCE_WHY]
Read this before generating any certified processing or secure data destruction language. It decides the close calls.

A registrar review flagged STS city pages: a certification claim sitting beside a city name and a local address reads as though the local site is certified. Only two STS sites are certified processing facilities, Jacksonville and Houston, Texas. Every other STS location is a collection and service point. STS IS certified processing and is still allowed to say so; what is not allowed is leaving it ambiguous where the certification lives.

THE RULE. On any page that targets a city and carries a local address, a certification claim is only acceptable if the sentence making the claim also names Jacksonville or Houston in that same sentence. Not the paragraph, not a nearby block, the same sentence.

TWO JOBS, AND DOING ONLY THE FIRST IS A FAILURE.
One: stop implying local certification.
Two: keep the page reading like what STS actually is, a credentialed operator running since 2011 across all 50 states. A page that comes out reading as though STS holds no certifications has FAILED, even if it contains zero instances of certified processing.

Both failure directions are real and both already happened on this project:
- A certifications FAQ answered with no certification in it. Empty answer reads as uncertified; worse than the original problem.
- A fabricated "Per EPA 40 CFR 261" citation swapped in to replace certified processing. A plausible but wrong citation on a compliance page during a certification review is the worst possible output.

The approach is volume plus clarity: cut in-scope mentions way down, make the few survivors fully location-clear, and give everything else approved wording that carries the same meaning without naming the scheme. The full allocation, vocabulary, exemptions, and the mandatory callout are in the Gate 6 certification LOCATION CLARITY rule. Never invent or substitute a different standard to fill a gap: drop the citation and state the fact plainly, or make it location-clear.

NEGATIVE SCOPE — STS doesn't offer: "paper shredding", "document shredding", "file destruction", "records shredding" → Replace with "hard drive destruction" or "digital media destruction"

EM DASHES. Never use em dashes (the literal — character) or the &mdash; HTML entity in generated page output. This applies to all body paragraphs, headlines, FAQ answers, CTA copy, and image alt text. Use commas, semicolons, periods, or parentheses for the same separation function. The SEO Audit catches em dashes downstream, but they are a model generation artifact that must be prevented at the source. This rule applies to page OUTPUT only; the prompt's own instructional prose is unaffected.

PROMOTIONAL TONE. Keep page copy factual and restrained. Forbidden in output: all-caps promotional phrases ("ABSOLUTELY FREE", "100% FREE", "BEST IN CLASS"), exclamation-driven promo, and unhedged superlatives ("the best", "the only", "guaranteed lowest", "unbeatable"). Shouty promotional tone is a 2026 E-E-A-T and AI-citation risk: answer engines and Google's current enforcement reward plain, hedged, factual phrasing over sales language. Approved: "free pickup for qualifying volumes", "trusted by", "certified", factual benefit statements. This is distinct from the COMPLIANCE CLAIMS LANGUAGE rule above (which governs regulatory claims); this rule governs promotional register. Applies to page OUTPUT only.

COMPLIANCE CLAIMS LANGUAGE. STS content describes compliance support, never guaranteed regulatory outcomes. This applies to every niche, with highest priority on healthcare, financial, and government pages where compliance content is densest.
FORBIDDEN: "guarantees compliance", "guarantees HIPAA compliance", "ensures compliance", "meets all {regulation} requirements", "fully compliant", "100% compliant", or any phrasing that promises a regulatory outcome.
APPROVED: "helps meet {regulation} requirements", "supports {regulation} compliance", "designed to support compliance with {regulation}", "documentation that supports {regulation} audits".
ABSOLUTE PROMISES: replace "always", "guaranteed", and "same-day guaranteed" with "typically", "generally", or "most organizations". Existing timeline specifics from Gate 14 (e.g. "same-week pickup", "Certificate within 48 hours") are factual service descriptions and remain approved.
PRICING: "free pickup" is permitted because pickup is genuinely free. Any broader free-service claim must be qualified, e.g. "free recycling for qualifying volumes". Never state specific prices.

CERTIFICATION SCOPE. State each certification only in its correct scope. Conflated certification claims are factually wrong and undermine the authority the page is built to project.
secure data destruction: data destruction only. Correct usage: "secure data destruction certified data destruction", "secure data destruction certified hard drive shredding". Forbidden: "secure data destruction certified recycling", "secure data destruction certified electronics recycling".
certified processing: electronics recycling and responsible downstream processing only. Correct SCOPE usage (recycling context, not data destruction): "certified processing", "certified processing electronics recycling". Forbidden scope error: "certified processing data destruction", "certified processing shredding". NOTE: scope-correct is not the same as location-clear. Any certified processing claim in visible page text must also satisfy the certification LOCATION CLARITY rule below, which is why bare "certified processing facility" next to a city is not usable in output; it appears only in a location-clear survivor naming Jacksonville or Houston.
NIST 800-88: a data sanitization standard, not a certification STS holds. Correct usage: "destruction that exceeds NIST 800-88", "NIST 800-88 compliant destruction". Forbidden: "NIST 800-88 certified".
When one sentence spans both destruction and recycling, name both certifications in their correct scope. Do not blend them into a single claim.

certification LOCATION CLARITY. Enforces the registrar location-clarity finding (see [R2_secure data destruction_COMPLIANCE_WHY]). Scope: certification only, in visible page text only.
